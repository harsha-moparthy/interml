**Permutation importance measures a model's reliance on a feature; partial dependence summarizes how predictions vary as that feature is changed in a synthetic evaluation.** Neither is automatically a causal effect. Correlated predictors can make both explanations misleading. [1][2]

For permutation importance, compute a baseline score on held-out data, shuffle one feature column, and measure the score decrease. Repeat shuffling to see variability. If the score barely changes, the model may not rely on that feature in this evaluation—but another correlated feature may supply nearly the same information.

Imagine income and a second field that is an almost exact copy of income. Either can substitute for the other, so individually shuffling one may show little importance. This does not establish that income-related information is irrelevant. Consider grouped permutation for the pair, while stating that it answers a different question about their combined contribution. [1]

Partial dependence for feature $j$ averages predictions after replacing that feature with a chosen value:

$$PD_j(z)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f(z,x_{i,-j}).$$

If age and years of work experience are correlated, setting age to 18 while retaining 30 years of experience creates unrealistic combinations. The resulting curve can reflect extrapolation outside the observed data rather than meaningful behavior for real people. [2]

Use individual conditional expectation curves to reveal heterogeneous model responses, inspect feature dependence, and restrict interpretation to supported regions. Conditional or accumulated-local approaches can address some issues but make different assumptions. For importance, always state the evaluation metric and dataset: a feature can matter for recall while appearing less important for accuracy.

**Interview takeaway:** importance is about predictive behavior under a defined perturbation; causal impact requires assumptions about interventions and the data-generating process. Even a stable, visually convincing plot should be checked against data support, model quality, and the decision it is intended to inform. [3][4]
