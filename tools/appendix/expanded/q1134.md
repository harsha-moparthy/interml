**Offline RL learns from a fixed dataset without collecting new exploratory interactions during training.** Its central difficulty is that the learned policy may prefer actions poorly represented in that dataset, where value estimates are unreliable. [1]

Imagine hospital logs in which a certain treatment was almost never given to a particular patient group. A learned value function might assign that treatment a high score by extrapolation. Deploying the policy to test that optimistic guess can be costly or unsafe; the missing outcomes cannot be recovered simply by training longer.

Bootstrapped value learning amplifies this issue. A Q-learning target contains $r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')$. The maximization can select an unsupported action whose value is accidentally too high. That optimistic target then updates earlier states, spreading the error through training.

Common responses include constraining the policy toward the behavior represented in the data, penalizing unsupported actions, and learning conservative value estimates. Conservative Q-Learning, for example, discourages excessively high estimated values for actions relative to those supported by the dataset. These approaches trade potential improvement against uncertainty; they do not make arbitrary extrapolation reliable. [2]

Behavior cloning is a useful baseline: it imitates logged actions directly and avoids some bootstrapping errors, but it also imitates suboptimal choices and may fail when its own mistakes lead to unfamiliar states. Compare offline RL with this baseline before claiming that a more complex objective is necessary. [1][3]

**Evaluation remains difficult.** A replay dataset does not reveal the outcome of every alternative action. Off-policy estimators require assumptions such as action support and appropriate treatment of confounding or state information. Use uncertainty estimates and clearly state where the new policy departs from observed behavior. Dataset coverage, reward quality, and the deployment setting often matter more than selecting a fashionable algorithm. A high estimated return on the same fixed logs is not proof of real-world improvement.
